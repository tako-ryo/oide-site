Visa and timing

Three ways in. The school files the paperwork; I tell you what your case needs and check nothing is missing.

Up to 90 days: usually no visa

Canada, US, UK, Australia, NZ and most of Europe enter visa-free. Short courses are fine; working isn't.

Start: 1 to 2 months before you fly.

Longer: student visa

Six months to two years, full-time. The school applies for your Certificate of Eligibility (COE); you take it to your embassy for the visa. Part-time work up to 28 hours a week.

Start: 6 to 9 months before. Intakes in April and October, and at many schools July and January.

Working holiday: study and work

Aged 18 to 30 with a Canadian, British, Australian, NZ, Irish, French or German passport (and some others)? A working-holiday visa lets you take a course and work, with no COE. Not open to US citizens.

Start: 3 to 6 months before. Some countries have quotas and application windows.

Student visa: what you'll need

  • Finished high school. Most schools ask for 12 years of schooling and 18 or over on arrival.
  • Some Japanese already. JLPT N5, JFT-Basic A1, or an interview with the school. You may have read "150 hours of study" elsewhere; the current rule is about your level, not hours.
  • Money for the course. If your passport is from the US, Canada, UK, Australia, NZ, Ireland, Germany, France or Brazil and the school is in immigration's top two tiers, you usually don't need a bank statement or a sponsor letter at all.
  • A school in good standing with immigration (適正校). The others don't go on your list.

Each school confirms its own list. You'll know exactly what your case needs before you pay anything.

Questions people ask me

Is it really free?

Yes. The school pays me a referral fee from its marketing budget. You never pay me, and your tuition is unchanged.

Do you only work with certain schools?

No. If the right school doesn't pay me, I'll tell you, and you apply directly.

Do I need to speak Japanese already?

No. Schools have beginner classes, and we talk in English.

I'm already talking to another agency. Can I still ask?

Yes. A second opinion costs nothing, and I won't contact a school unless you ask.

Can I come as a tourist and switch to a student visa in Japan?

No. Immigration doesn't normally allow a change from a 90-day stay to a student visa. You go home, the school gets your COE, and you come back with the visa. Plan for it from the start.

Can I apply to several schools at once?

For a student-visa course, one at a time: the COE is tied to a single school. That's why the shortlist comes first. Short courses are more flexible.

Is there an age limit?

Student-visa courses: 18 and over at most schools, with no upper limit; over 30, the school may ask for a short study plan. Under 18: some schools take short courses with a guardian's consent. Ask me.

What happens after the course?

Three common paths: a job in Japan (most employers want JLPT N2 and a degree), a university or vocational school here, or home with Japanese you can actually use. I ask about this on day one, because it changes which school fits.

How do I get the Tako Talk coupon?

Once your school confirms your enrolment, I send you the 3-month code by WhatsApp or email. It's for your own account only.

Can I work while I study?

On a student visa, up to 28 hours a week. On a 90-day tourist stay, no.

Do you help with housing?

I'll explain each school's options and the paperwork in English. The contract is between you and the school.

You're in Japan, not in my country. Isn't that harder?

The opposite. I speak to the school in Japanese during its hours, and I'm awake when you need me after you land.

Are you an official agent?

I'm an independent Japanese teacher in Japan, working in English and Japanese. Introductions are referral arrangements. For visa questions beyond the basics, I'll point you to the school or the official source.
